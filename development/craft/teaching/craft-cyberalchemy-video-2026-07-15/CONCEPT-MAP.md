# Epistemic Concept Map

## Governing Question

How can we know that what we made preserves what we meant?

The video answers this through four questions for each concept:

1. What is it?
2. How does it participate in knowing?
3. What visible event makes the distinction necessary?
4. What should it not be confused with?

## Concept Sequence

| Order | Concept | Working definition | Epistemic role | Visual witness | Not the same as |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Intention | A desired change or possibility that does not yet specify all conditions of realization. | Supplies direction, but not enough structure to prove what should be built. | A person points through a wall where passage is needed. | A prompt, requirement list, or plan. |
| 2 | Objective | The current statement of the problem the work is trying to solve. | Makes success discussable and revisable. | "Create passage between rooms" replaces "build a door." | The artifact chosen to pursue it. |
| 3 | Boundary and context | The conditions inside which a claim or artifact is expected to hold. | Determines which facts count and prevents local success from masquerading as system success. | Room, wall, corridor, traffic direction, and fire exit appear around the door. | Background detail or unlimited context. |
| 4 | Schema | A chosen structure that makes selected distinctions in an intention legible enough to translate. | Determines what the builder can preserve and what validation can inspect. | A blueprint contains width and height but initially omits passage direction. | Reality itself, a neutral mirror, or merely a file format. |
| 5 | Translator | The composite process that attempts to carry structure from schema into an artifact. | Explains why fidelity depends on tools, context, vocabulary, attention, and feedback, not only instructions. | Builder, model, tools, materials, and context form one transparent translation chain. | A deterministic compiler or the LLM alone. |
| 6 | Artifact | A concrete result that can now be inspected, used, and challenged. | Externalizes the current model so reality can answer it. | The finished door swings smoothly into a wall. | Proof that the objective was achieved. |
| 7 | Validation | A comparison between artifact, schema, objective, and governing conditions. | Converts "looks right" into explicit evidence and reveals where the comparison is too narrow. | Hinge and latch checks pass; passage test fails. | Testing only the component or confirming the original plan. |
| 8 | Residue | A meaningful mismatch, loss, ambiguity, or unexplained obligation left by translation and validation. | Carries new information about the inadequacy of the current representation or route. | A red tracing line marks the blocked path through the doorway. | Waste, generic error, or a reason to restart automatically. |
| 9 | Structured tension | A deliberate challenge to the current artifact, boundary, or claim. | Searches for hidden assumptions before they harden into authority. | A second person attempts to move a box through the door and exposes the swing conflict. | Negativity, stylistic criticism, or endless debate. |
| 10 | Smallest coherent unit | The smallest unit that still preserves meaning, one responsibility, inputs, outputs, validation, failure behavior, and a path back upward. | Reduces relation and attention load without destroying the knowledge needed for local judgment. | The hinge-and-swing problem is isolated with wall, door, clearance, and return interface intact. | The smallest possible task or arbitrary fragmentation. |
| 11 | Craft layer | A provisional schema-and-artifact context created when responsible closure cannot happen in the current layer. | Turns unresolved residue into a bounded object of inquiry and work. | The hinge problem opens as a nested workbench only after local adjustment fails. | Any subtask, folder, or decomposition. |
| 12 | Recomposition | The proven return of a locally validated result into its upper artifact and objective. | Tests whether local knowledge remains valid in the whole system. | The corrected hinge and swing return to the doorway; the passage test runs again. | Copying a component back or declaring the subtask done. |
| 13 | Trace | The preserved evidence, decisions, rejected alternatives, residue, and next route of the work. | Makes knowledge durable beyond the memory of the current actor or conversation. | Blueprint layers retain why the swing changed and which test failed. | Administrative history or an activity log without reasons. |
| 14 | Route and ownership | The explicit authority responsible for the next lifecycle action or decision. | Prevents evidence from silently becoming a decision and working material from becoming authority. | The unresolved fire-safety question leaves the workbench and routes to its qualified owner. | Assignment convenience or file proximity. |
| 15 | Reflection | The decision about whether observed residue should change the current artifact, method, vocabulary, validation, or future route. | Converts repeated experience into candidate learning while preserving the promotion boundary. | Several red trace layers are compared; a repeated omission changes the future doorway schema. | A retrospective summary or automatic self-improvement. |
| 16 | Craft | The recursive method for turning intention into a stable artifact by moving between schema and data until a coherent unit is executed, validated, and recomposed with acceptable residue. | Treats making as an iterative knowledge process rather than one-way execution. | The complete door sequence runs as a loop with repair, descent, return, and stop edges. | A project-management workflow, a prompt recipe, or universal proof theory. |
| 17 | CyberAlchemy | Governed synthesis that turns vague intent and discovered evidence into navigable, reviewable, reusable artifacts through objective, discovery, tension, trace, validation, and lifecycle routing. | Governs how the Craft loop becomes inspectable capability rather than private improvisation. | The door loop zooms out into objective, artifact, discovery, tension, trace, and route anchors. | Another step inside Craft or a claim of autonomous machine wisdom. |

## Relationship Between Craft And CyberAlchemy

Craft explains the recursive epistemic engine of making:

```text
intention -> schema -> translation -> artifact -> validation -> residue
                         ^                              |
                         |------ repair or layer ------|
```

CyberAlchemy governs the conditions under which that engine can responsibly
operate and persist:

```text
objective + bounded context + discovery + tension
  -> reviewable artifact + validation + trace + route + reflection
```

Craft without CyberAlchemy can learn locally and still lose its reasons,
authority boundary, or continuation path. CyberAlchemy without Craft can
govern artifacts while leaving the mechanics of schema-to-artifact learning
implicit. The video presents them as complementary, not interchangeable.

## Honesty Boundaries

- "Schema" and "instance" have formal uses in category-theoretic database
  work. Craft borrows the structural intuition and generalizes it
  operationally; the generalization is an analogy, not a theorem.
- "Translation entropy" is qualitative in this beginner-facing video. No
  numerical estimator or threshold is claimed.
- A smallest coherent unit is an operational judgment under a current
  objective, not a globally unique mathematical object.
- Residue can be informative without being good. Safety defects and failed
  obligations still require repair or blocking decisions.
- The door is an authorial instrument. It demonstrates the distinctions; it
  does not empirically validate Craft.
- The video may call Craft a working epistemology of making, but not a proven
  universal physics of creation.
