Read The Ledger First

The Craft ledger is not a status board. It is a structured record of the work state. A team can ask the ledger: what context are we in, what artifact changed, what relation matters, what blocker or enabler exists, what decision was made, and what next move is allowed.

Core reading

The ledger answers concrete questions.

  • Where is this work located in the larger effort?
  • Which artifact, decision, blocker, gate, or enabler changed?
  • Which lane must judge or review the change?
  • What evidence makes the status believable?
  • What parent context or next move is affected?

Five Row Families

The current MVP ledger has five row families. They are deliberately plain: enough structure for humans to read, enough schema discipline for future validation and indexing.

contexts

Where the work lives

A context row names the parent, purpose, stage, gate, next move, and owned artifacts. This is how projects inside projects stay visible.

artifacts

What exists

An artifact row points to a file, work-pack, schema, validation report, receipt, handoff, or source baseline owned by a context.

relations

How things connect

A relation row says one thing contains, blocks, enables, depends on, informs, or supersedes another thing.

typed items

What needs attention

A typed item row records a blocker, gate, or enabler with type, lane, source, target, status, evidence, and closure condition.

decisions

What authority chose

A decision row records selections, waivers, approvals, deferrals, and rejections so exceptions do not disappear into memory.

how to read a row

Start with the ID, then follow the references.

  • CTX-* rows tell you the work boundary and next move.
  • ART-* rows tell you which file or receipt carries evidence.
  • REL-* rows tell you why one context affects another.
  • BLK-*, GATE-*, and ENA-* rows tell you what is blocked, checked, or unlocked.
  • DEC-* rows tell you when a human or governance choice changed the path.

Lane Fields

Lanes are fields on typed ledger rows. They say what kind of judgment the row needs before naming a specific person, team, or agent.

business Intent, value, scope, acceptance meaning, trade-offs, and user impact.
tech Architecture, feasibility, implementation design, data shape, and integration.
qa Test planning, regression safety, acceptance readiness, and defect confirmation.
validator Independent confirmation that evidence satisfies the current gate.
auditor Traceability, provenance, promotion evidence, and process conformance.
governance Ownership, authority, promotion permission, policy, and route legitimacy.
planner Sequence, dependencies, SWU/task breakdown, and cross-context coordination.
operations Tooling, runtime access, environment constraints, budget, compute, and availability.
integrator Recomposition between child work, parent contexts, and downstream contracts.
blocker_refiner Clarifies raw blockers before anyone claims resolution.

Blocker Layer

A blocker is not just a red status. In Craft it has a base type, lane ownership, source, target, status, evidence, closure condition, and sometimes a waiver decision.

1

raw

Something is stuck, but the cause or owner is still unclear.

2

typed

The blocker gets a base condition type and primary lane.

3

refined

Evidence, closure condition, source, target, and route are explicit.

4

proposed

A responsible lane claims a possible resolution with evidence.

5

resolved

Closure is allowed only when evidence or an explicit waiver exists.

Raw-to-resolved is prohibited. That rule is the speed governor that keeps agentic work from producing false closure faster than people can detect it.

Enabler Layer

Enablers are the positive side of the same system. They say which artifact, decision, validation, resource, or design structure opens the next move for another context.

schema

Design enabler

A structured schema makes fixture rows consistent enough for review, validation, and later indexing.

evidence

Validation enabler

Examples, tests, review notes, and receipts let validators decide pass, flag, block, deferred, or waived.

sequence

Sequence enabler

A completed SWU or work-pack unlocks the next child context and updates the parent next move.

How A Team Uses The Ledger

The organization model is simpler when everything starts as ledger maintenance. Every meeting, review, agent run, or handoff should either update a row, add evidence to a row, close a row, or create the next row.

non-technical lanes

Business and governance can own real state.

They define context purpose, acceptance meaning, trade-off decisions, waivers, promotion permission, and priority intent without editing code or pretending to validate implementation details.

technical lanes

Developers keep implementation from swallowing authority.

They own architecture, interfaces, schema shape, integration, recomposition, runtime constraints, and the technical part of closure evidence.

agent lanes

Agents produce bounded moves, not invisible certainty.

An agent run should create or update artifacts, surface blockers, propose enablers, and attach evidence. It should not silently close raw blockers or promote candidate authority.

review lanes

QA, validator, and auditor protect throughput.

Their job is not to slow every move. It is to make closure cheap to trust by checking evidence, lane ownership, decision provenance, and recomposition.

1

Open a context

Name the parent, purpose, stage, gate, next move, and owned artifacts.

2

Assign lanes

Use lanes for responsibility type before assigning specific people or agents.

3

Refine blockers

Turn vague stoppage into typed, lane-owned, evidence-backed rows.

4

Record enablers

Show which artifact, decision, validation, or resource unlocks what.

5

Validate evidence

Use pass, flag, block, waived, and deferred as real review outcomes.

6

Recompose

Close the child unit only after the parent context and next move are updated.

Evidence Boundary

This x-ray separates facts directly grounded in Craft artifacts from the applied operating model for organizations.

source-backed

Directly grounded facts

  • README.md states Craft is candidate, local, checkpoint-first, and not canonical authority.
  • CRAFT-LEDGER-SCHEMA.yml defines row families, enums, operational lanes, validation rules, and blocker lifecycle rules.
  • LEDGER.md instantiates contexts, artifacts, relations, typed items, and decisions.
  • LEDGER-VALIDATION.md reports pass for current schema and blocker lifecycle checks.
  • CRAFT-ARCHITECTURE.md defines Craft as an orchestrating method that routes to existing Arcanum capabilities without replacing their authority.
applied interpretation

Organization-use model

  • Use lanes as a shared language for mixed technical and non-technical responsibility.
  • Treat agent runs as ledger mutations plus evidence, not as free-floating chat conclusions.
  • Use blocker refinement as the minimum safety rule for fast parallel work.
  • Keep role delegation automation manual until repeated ledger states prove the mappings.
  • Keep promotion, runtime mutation, indexes, and scoring gated by explicit evidence and authority.
