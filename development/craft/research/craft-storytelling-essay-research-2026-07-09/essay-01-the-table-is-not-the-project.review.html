Which table?

p008 core_insight_language_becomes_executable_through_ai line 28

A dining table is not a desk. A desk is not a coffee table. A work table for a garage is not a small table for a bedroom. Even a small work desk is not yet specific enough. Is it for a laptop? For drawing? For a child? Does it have to fit beside a bed? Does the floor slope? Should it be cheap, beautiful, strong, portable, repairable, or fast to build?

The schema is the chosen shape of valid work. The artifact is the thing that actually exists. Craft lives in the disciplined movement between them. We choose a structure, produce something from it, compare the result back, and learn from what does not match.

Maybe one leg is slightly short. Maybe the surface flexes. Maybe the desk fits the room but blocks the closet. Maybe the wood was cheaper but splinters more. This leftover is not shame. It is information. Craft calls this residue: the meaningful mismatch, missing detail, ambiguity, or validation gap left after the translation from intent to artifact.

Residue asks for a route. Some residue is a repair: add a foot pad to the short leg. Some residue asks for more research: why does this material flex? Some residue asks for redesign: the leg position is wrong. Some residue asks for a new plan. Some residue tells us that a step we thought was simple is actually its own hidden project.
