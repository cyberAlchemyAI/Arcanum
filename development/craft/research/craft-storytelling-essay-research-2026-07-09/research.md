## Explorer 1: Alexander, Christopher

Angle: concrete object / pattern-language story from table and built-environment
corpus; methodology narrative and spatial resonance.

### Strongest Arc

Start with a table, not Craft. The table is familiar enough that students think
they understand it, then the reveal is: "table" is not a buildable instruction.
It hides use, place, height, materials, tools, joinery, sequence, checks, and
failure modes. The object becomes a pattern-language doorway: a visible thing
whose invisible structure has to be named before it can be responsibly made.
Cite: `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.md`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S01`;
`CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.yml`, `opening_rule`.

### Narrative Moves

- Pause / reveal: Show the plain table and wait. Let students list what it hides
  before giving buckets. The discovery should feel earned, not explained at
  them. Cite: `CRAFT-INTRO-S01`.
- Definition: "Which table?" A dining table, desk, coffee table, and work table
  are different targets. Definition names the object and its boundary. Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S02`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Craft Cycle / Define`.
- Research: The room, user, material, and examples can change the design.
  Research is not decoration; it is the facts that would change the object.
  Cite: `CRAFT-INTRO-S03`; `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.yml`,
  `beginner_constraints`.
- Schema: The plan drawing becomes "the chosen shape of valid work": size,
  material, joinery, finish. This is where pattern language becomes operational:
  the visible table is governed by a chosen hidden structure. Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S04`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Initial Vocabulary /
  Schema`.
- Plan: Design is still not execution. Break it into measure, cut, join,
  finish, check. The plan asks: what is the next executable piece? Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S05`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Craft Cycle / Plan`.
- Artifact: The drawing cannot hold coffee; the table can. The artifact is the
  thing that now exists and can be inspected. Cite: `CRAFT-INTRO-S06`;
  `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Initial Vocabulary / Data`.
- Validation: Compare the made table back to the chosen structure: height,
  level, stability, fit. "Validation is not a vibe" is the clean teaching line.
  Cite: `CRAFT-INTRO-S07`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Craft Cycle /
  Validate`.
- Residue: A wobble is not shame; it is information. The leftover tells whether
  to repair, research again, redesign, re-plan, or split a new layer. Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S08`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Initial Vocabulary /
  Residue`.
- Craft layer: The ornate leg reveal is the strongest pattern-language turn.
  "Make the legs" looked like one step until carving, proportion, tools,
  practice, and checks appeared inside it. A step becomes its own build when it
  needs its own lifecycle and a way back into the whole. Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S09`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S10`;
  `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.yml`, `teaching_promise`.
- SCU: The beginner rule: not whole app, not one blue button, but the smallest
  responsible piece that can be built, checked, and recomposed. Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S12`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Initial Vocabulary /
  Smallest Coherent Unit`.
- Entropy: Use "dashboard" or "table" as the drift example: too many good
  answers compete when target, user, data, actions, and done criteria are loose.
  Cite: `CRAFT-INTRO-S13`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Entropy, SCU, And
  PCRA Translation`.
- Final rule: Recompose: Craft is the loop that finds the next responsible
  schema/data layer needed to turn intent into artifact with acceptable residue.
  For beginners, say: "If a step needs its own plan, tools, validation, and way
  back into the whole, it has become its own craft layer." Cite:
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S14`; `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.yml`,
  `teaching_promise`; `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Conversation Synthesis`.

### Risks

- The table metaphor can overexplain if every concept gets forced back into
  carpentry. Use it to earn the pattern, then move to software at
  `CRAFT-INTRO-S11`.
- "Residue" can become sentimental if framed as wisdom or beauty. Keep it
  operational: mismatch, missing detail, next responsible move.
- "Pattern" can sound mystical. Keep it physical: dimensions, use, material,
  sequence, checks, recomposition.
- "SCU" can be misheard as "smallest possible piece." The source says the
  smallest possible unit can lose meaning; the useful unit is small enough to
  check and large enough to fit back into the whole.
- "Entropy" should not become a physics lecture. For beginners, keep the slide
  language: "too many possible meanings still competing."

## Explorer 2: Halford, Graeme

Angle: beginner cognition / cognitive-load story from learner understanding
corpus; methodology instructional clarity.

An absolute beginner should experience the table lesson as a sequence of
recognition, not theory. The first feeling should be: "I know what a table is...
wait, actually I need more information." That is the right cognitive doorway for
Craft. Start with the familiar object, let ambiguity become visible, then
introduce each term only after the learner has already felt the need for it.
This matches the deck rule: concrete example first, one new term per slide, no
formal jargon up front. See `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.md`,
`Beginner Constraints`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S01`.

### Table Lesson Flow

Pause longest on `CRAFT-INTRO-S01`. The 15-second silent list is load-bearing:
beginners need to generate "materials, tools, measurements, steps" themselves
before Craft names anything. If the presenter reveals the buckets too fast, the
lesson becomes vocabulary transfer instead of discovery.

Reveal clues in this order:

1. Object ambiguity: "Which table?" before any method term. This earns `define`
   in `CRAFT-INTRO-S02`.
2. Missing facts: "What would change the design?" This earns `research` in
   `CRAFT-INTRO-S03`.
3. Chosen structure: only after research, show the annotated plan and earn
   `schema` in `CRAFT-INTRO-S04`.
4. Work order: earn `plan` through next action, input, output, and done check in
   `CRAFT-INTRO-S05`.
5. Real thing: earn `artifact` by contrasting sketch vs table in
   `CRAFT-INTRO-S06`.
6. Comparison: earn `validate` as matching the thing back to the chosen
   structure in `CRAFT-INTRO-S07`.
7. Leftover: earn `residue` only after a mismatch exists in `CRAFT-INTRO-S08`.

The term ladder is good, but `schema` is the first danger point. Say "the chosen
shape of valid work" before saying schema, exactly as the schema file instructs.
The second danger point is `residue`: beginners may hear it as "failure." The
slide already protects against this with "Anything left over is not shame. It is
information." Keep that sentence. It is doing real instructional work. See
`CRAFT-INTRO-S04`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S08`, and `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`,
`Initial Vocabulary / Residue`.

### Where Cognitive Load Breaks

The presentation will break if it compresses `Define -> Research -> Design` into
"make a plan." Beginners with vibe-coding experience often jump from wish to
prompt to output. The deck's strongest correction is making research visible
before design. Do not skip `CRAFT-INTRO-S03`; it is the anti-vibe-drift slide.

It will also break if recursion appears before the simple table lifecycle lands.
The ornate table in `CRAFT-INTRO-S09` works only after learners have already
validated and reflected on a simple artifact. Otherwise "craft layer" becomes
abstract architecture talk. The teaching promise says the learner should
understand: "If a step needs its own plan, tools, validation, and way back into
the whole, it has become its own craft layer." That must feel obvious before it
becomes a term. See `Teaching Promise`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S09`,
`CRAFT-INTRO-S10`.

`SCU` and `entropy` should stay late. They are explanatory compression, not early
teaching concepts. `CRAFT-INTRO-S12` should feel like "what size can I
responsibly build and check?" before naming SCU. `CRAFT-INTRO-S13` should feel
like "my vague prompt gave the AI too many good answers" before naming entropy.
This directly matches the initial definition's warning that too-small units lose
meaning and too-large units lose fidelity. See `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`,
`Entropy, SCU, And PCRA Translation`.

### Three Essay Directions

1. "The Table Is Not The Project" - Best companion for the first half of the
   deck. It follows the existing essay schema: table ambiguity, research,
   schema, plan, artifact, validation, residue. This supports `CRAFT-INTRO-S01`
   through `CRAFT-INTRO-S08`.
2. "When One Step Becomes Its Own Build" - Best bridge essay for recursion. It
   can use the ornate leg to show why "make the legs" may require its own
   define, research, design, plan, build, validate, reflect loop. This supports
   `CRAFT-INTRO-S09` and `CRAFT-INTRO-S10`.
3. "Why Vibe Coding Drifts" - Best bridge into software. It should explain that
   vague feature names like "dashboard" or "login" contain too many possible
   meanings until the learner names user, data, actions, checks, and success
   criteria. This supports `CRAFT-INTRO-S11` through `CRAFT-INTRO-S14` and ties
   back to residue as information rather than failure.

Net: the deck is strongest when it teaches Craft as a learner's felt reduction
of confusion: first name the thing, then learn the facts, then choose the shape,
then build only the next responsible piece.

## Explorer 3: Spivak, David

Angle: formal Craft definition from schema/data/functorial analogy corpus;
methodology formal fidelity.

### Non-Negotiable Spine

Craft is not "planning" or "creative process." It is the recursive search for
the next smallest coherent schema/data layer needed to translate intent into
artifact with acceptable residue. Source: `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`,
`Executive Definition`, `Conversation Synthesis`.

The core morphism is:

```text
Intent -> chosen schema -> functor-like translator -> data/artifact
       -> residue -> absorb/split/route/promote
       -> next schema/data layer when needed
```

Source: `CRAFT-INITIAL-DEFINITION.md`, `Loop 2: Stabilize The Formal Spine`.

The formal analogy must stay disciplined: "functor-like" means
structure-preserving translation from schema to data, not a claim of perfect
mathematical functoriality. The translator is composite: human intent, LLM
behavior, harness, tools, context, local vocabulary, validation, and feedback.
For LLM work it is probabilistic, contextual, relational, and attentive. Source:
`Functor-Like Translator`, `Entropy, SCU, And PCRA Translation`.

Schema is the chosen structure that makes intent legible and constrains valid
instances. Data/artifact is the produced, populated, observed, or executed
counterpart. Validation is the comparison of artifact back to schema. Source:
`Initial Vocabulary`; slide ids `CRAFT-INTRO-S04`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S06`,
`CRAFT-INTRO-S07`.

Residue is not generic failure. It is meaningful mismatch, loss, ambiguity,
contradiction, missing structure, scope pressure, or validation gap after
translation. It must be routed: repair, research, redesign, re-plan, split a
layer, promote, or stop. Source: `Residue`, `Residue Classification`; slide
`CRAFT-INTRO-S08`.

SCU is the smallest coherent unit that still has meaning, one responsibility,
inputs, outputs, validation/review surface, failure behavior, and recomposition
path. SWU is the executable planning instance of SCU: assignable, runnable,
validatable, and recomposable into task/wave/plan/artifact. Source: `Smallest
Coherent Unit`, `Single Work Unit`; slide `CRAFT-INTRO-S12`.

Recomposition is load-bearing. A unit that works locally but cannot reattach to
the upper schema has recomposition residue. Splitting only helps while residue
decreases and recomposition remains meaningful. Source: `Residue
Classification`, `Reflection Tower Method`, `Stop Criteria`.

Reflection tower is the recursive structure where residue from one schema/data
translation becomes seed data for a new schema. It stops at acceptable residue
for the current objective, not universal completeness. Source: `Reflection
Tower`, `Reflection Tower Method`.

Craft layer is the moment a step needs its own plan, tools, validation, and way
back into the whole. This must be earned through the table/ornate-leg story
before software transfer. Source: `CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.md`,
`Teaching Promise`; slides `CRAFT-INTRO-S09`, `CRAFT-INTRO-S10`.

The public story sequence should preserve: table recognition -> define ->
research -> schema/design -> plan -> artifact -> validate -> residue -> ornate
leg recursion -> software feature recursion -> SCU -> entropy -> Craft
recomposition. Source: essay companion schema in
`CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.md` and
`CRAFT-CODING-CLASS-SLIDE-SCHEMA.yml`.

### Collapse Warnings

- Reject any essay that makes Craft sound like generic planning. Planning is
  only one phase: `Define -> Design -> Plan -> Execute -> Validate -> Reflect`.
- Reject generic design-thinking language if it loses schema/data translation,
  residue classification, validation, recomposition, and layer recursion.
- Reject vague creativity framing. Craft is not "be inspired and iterate"; it is
  governed translation under lossy conditions.
- Reject any story that jumps from "table idea" directly to "build plan." The
  schema requires define and research before design; otherwise the formal method
  collapses into a reveal trick.
- Reject "make it smaller" as the universal answer. Sometimes smaller units
  increase residue; the right move may be a missing schema, relation,
  validation, or lifecycle owner.
