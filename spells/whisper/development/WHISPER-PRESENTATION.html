Whisper

Spell design presentation

Whisper turns writing into a governed creative lifecycle.

It takes raw author intent, extracts a small substrate, chooses narrative primitives, plans the text, drafts it, validates it, and keeps learning residue for the next piece.

The problem

Text generation usually jumps from idea to draft too quickly.

Hidden intent

The model guesses the real objective.

Is the author trying to clarify, persuade, inspire, recruit, fundraise, teach, or invite a conversation? Whisper names that before composing.

Mixed levels

Tone, audience, and structure get tangled.

Whisper separates felt meaning, contextual fit, and movement so the text is not just a polished style pass.

No lifecycle

Drafts rarely carry useful memory.

Each text leaves behind what worked, what failed, what assumptions mattered, and what the next transport should learn.

The object

The smallest coherent unit is the Text Intent Substrate.

text_intent_substrate:
  author_objective
  resonance_core
  relevance_core
  trajectory_core
  technique_stack

Not a style guide

It captures constraints that affect construction.

Audience, desired reader change, narrative anchor, body parts, ending strategy, length, and review criteria all belong here.

Not a full workflow

It is small enough to reuse.

The substrate can drive a Substack post now, then fundraising copy, articles, slide narratives, and longer works later.

Three SCU cores

Every candidate must balance one selection from each core.

resonance_core

What should it feel like?

Tone, voice, style register, emotional residue, value signal, and forbidden feels.

relevance_core

Who is it for and why will it matter?

Target public, reader state, domain, authority mode, assumptions, objections, and reader reward.

trajectory_core

What movement does it perform?

Objective, transport, narrative anchor, introduction, body parts, ending, length, and constraints.

The key design move: avoid optimizing three versions of tone. A viable candidate has to carry feeling, audience fit, and movement at the same time.

First proof

L0 starts with a Substack post for the research group.

Substack research post
Transport id: substack_research_post

This is the selected L0 proof transport.

Why this first
Why this first

It forces clarity, voice, research-group relevance, argument structure, and a humane ending without immediate conversion pressure.

Required body parts
Hook, research context, core insight, implications, invitation or next thread.

The post should help readers understand why the idea matters and what conversation should continue.

Default constraints
800-1600 words, evidence, soft invitation, clear living prose.

The style target is intellectually generous rather than sales-driven.

Process

The writing lifecycle is explicit, finite, and reviewable.

Raw intenttopic, rough thesis, notes, source material
Interrogateask one blocker question when needed
Substrateextract objective, cores, constraints
Tournamentcompare primitive and technique sets
Planchoose body parts and structure
Draftcompose against the plan
Residuevalidation, revision tasks, learned patterns

Next transport pressure

Fundraising comes next, but it should not distort L0.

Reuse

Fundraising still needs the same substrate.

It needs resonance, relevance, and trajectory: how it feels, who it speaks to, and what movement it asks the reader to make.

Extend

It adds trust, proof, urgency, identity, ask shape, and impact path.

Those fields should arrive as a transport extension after the Substack flow proves the base lifecycle.

If trust/proof/ask mechanics become repeatedly central, fundraising may earn its own core later. For now, it stays an extension boundary.

Start here

To run Whisper for the first post, answer one thing clearly.

Needed input

What is the concrete topic or thesis?

Example shape: write a Substack post for our research group about the idea that meaningful text should be developed like software, aimed at research collaborators, leaving them curious enough to help shape the first run.

Expected output

A substrate, a composition plan, then a draft.

The first artifact should be reviewable before it becomes prose, so the group can see the intent model and improve the text before publishing.
