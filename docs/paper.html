The paper

DomainSpec: deterministic software derivation and governance attenuation in multi-agent systems.

Vladimir Rondelli · Victor Boscaro

A layered meta-architecture that turns domain knowledge into verified software through deterministic derivation rules and self-regulating governance.

Abstract

LLM agents can now generate code, tests, and infrastructure from natural-language specifications. Without formal structure, their outputs stay stochastic — non-reproducible, non-auditable, ungovernable at scale. DomainSpec is a layered meta-architecture (L0–L7) that bridges domain knowledge to verified software through deterministic derivation rules and self-regulating governance. Four contributions: a dual-track meta-model spanning business semantics and operational enforcement; a typed ontology of 25 meta-types and 29 typed relationships that enables deterministic derivation per feature (T = f(C, R, Δ)) and across feature composition; a governance-attenuation theory explaining why adding governance layers to multi-agent systems decreases per-layer fidelity, bounded by channel capacity; and a meta-circular self-governance mechanism where the framework's axioms and tuning loop govern — and prune — themselves. We validate on a production system of 7 features across 3 pillars, deriving 445 test obligations and 146 observability metrics from 89 domain concepts.

The problem

Three things stay unsolved when agents write the software.

1

The stochastic gap

Same specification, different tests and implementations on each run. There is no deterministic function from specification to verification obligations.

2

The governance paradox

As you add rules and review layers to constrain agents, per-rule compliance decreases. More governance produces less governance.

3

The meta-circular problem

Who governs the governance? Frameworks that constrain agents must themselves be constrained — an infinite regress most approaches resolve by fiat.

Contributions

Derivation and governance are structural consequences of the layering, not bolt-ons.

C1 · Meta-architecture

Dual-track, seven layers

A business × operational model where each layer governs the one below and is governed by the one above, with derivation chains traceable from epistemic axioms to enforcement gates.

C2 · Derivation calculus

T = f(C, R, Δ)

A per-feature function plus a composition extension that map concepts, typed relationships, and rules to deterministic test obligations — and 16 symmetric rules that derive production metrics from the same documents.

C3 · Governance attenuation

Why fidelity decays with layers

A formal model of why governance fidelity drops as layers accumulate, and how structural interventions invert the trend without removing layers.

C4 · Meta-circular governance

A bounded strange loop

The framework governs itself: an axiom governs governance, a constitution rule prunes the constitution, and meta-health metrics observe the observer — resolving the regress by structure, not authority.

The seven layers

From ontology to orchestration.

  • L0–L1 — Ontology. A closed vocabulary of 25 meta-types and 29 typed relationships, including cross-feature composition edges.
  • L2 — Derived software. Tests, code, and metrics produced by deterministic rules over the L1 concepts.
  • L3–L4 — Governance. A formal constitution grounded in epistemic axioms, each rule traceable to the harm its absence causes.
  • L5 — Navigation. Structured discovery of concepts across the knowledge graph.
  • L6 — Compliance. Alignment audits, blocking gates, deterministic signal detection.
  • L7 — Orchestration. Specialized agents and skills coordinated through a staged pipeline.

Demonstrated results

The calculus is open. The engine that realizes it is the product.

A deterministic reference engine that realizes C2 has been built and validated. We report measured behavior; the derivation algorithm and the Lean validations are not disclosed here.

  • Determinism by construction. The same specification documents produce byte-identical test obligations across runs and machines — the property C2 predicts, without a stochastic generator.
  • Validated. The engine's suite passes (95 tests, 0 type errors); output is byte-stable; honesty and containment controls pass.
  • Refuses rather than fabricates. Where a spec is not formal enough to derive a checkable test, it emits a counted, visible gap — a spec-formalization metric, 96% on the pilot.
  • Fault detection. Mutation testing on the pilot: after formalizing one implicit rule, the derived suite's score rose 38.75% → 54.37% (hardest module doubled); the regenerated suite passed 13/13 against real, unmutated code. Single-feature pilot, not a corpus result.
  • Self-application. Run on its own specification, the engine derives its own obligations — a fixpoint.
