Saturn

A system that defends its own architecture.

Saturn treats domain intent as a reference baseline. It measures reality against that baseline, and it does not only report drift — it acts on it, turning governance into executable behavior. Most teams keep systems aligned by heroics. Saturn makes the alignment a loop.

The architecture of convergence

Intent flows down. Truth flows up.

Saturn runs as a lifecycle. Intent descends into execution; evidence rises to inform governance. The same loop closes on itself.

1

Observe

Read telemetry from what was actually built and run.

2

Compare it against the domain contracts — the intent baseline.

3

4

Execute the correction through the agentic substrate.

5

Verify

Confirm closure, then feed the result back into governance.

Four layers, one meta-loop

Saturn traverses every layer.

It reads telemetry from the perception layers, evaluates it against the intent layers, and triggers responses through the execution and regulation layers.

1 · Regulation & Interface

Where humans set direction

The human harness (priorities, dashboards) and governance (constants, quality gates).

2 · Modeling & Intent

Where meaning is fixed

Foundation (domain structures, ontologies) and formalization (feature contracts). This is the DomainSpec layer.

3 · Operation & Execution

Where the work runs

The agentic substrate (workflows, skills) and infrastructure (runtime, CI loops).

4 · Verification & Perception

Where truth is measured

Quality (deterministic tests) and observability (drift telemetry). This is where the audits live.

Status, honestly: Saturn is the convergence frame. Today the execution layer (Arcanum) is shipped publicly and DomainSpec's deterministic engine is built; the full closed loop is the intended target, not yet a shipped public surface.

Free & moat

The audits are free. The language and the proofs are the product.

Saturn's verification layer produces audits we offer openly. The deeper machinery — the formal language that makes derivation deterministic, and the Lean validations that make it provable — is the part we sell.

Free

The audits

  • Layering audit — does the system respect its own layer boundaries (domain depends on nothing; each layer only on the one below)?
  • Tag audit — does the code still trace to the spec it claims, with no orphaned or drifted tags?
  • Alignment audit — does what was built still match the domain intent, and where has it diverged?
  • LLM test generator — the free, open reference path from spec to tests.
The moat

Language & Lean validations

The DomainSpec language and its deterministic derivation, and the Lean formal validations that prove the obligations hold. We demonstrate the results — determinism (same docs in, byte-identical out), measured fault detection, machine-checked proofs — but the mechanism is the product, not published.

Open by design: the framework and calculus (the meta-types, the typed relationships, the derivation calculus) stay public. The optimized engine and the proofs are what you buy.
