# Categorical Lens

## Purpose

Inspect the object through relevant things, relations, transformations,
composition, interfaces, and what is preserved or lost when moving between
forms or levels.

Core question: **What are the relevant things, how are they related, and what is
preserved when we move or compose between them?**

## Investigation

Begin with the actual domain. Do not begin by naming objects, morphisms,
functors, adjunctions, fibers, or other formal structures.

Ask:

- What kinds of things need to remain distinguishable?
- Which relations are load-bearing?
- Which transformations move between forms, states, levels, or representations?
- Can transformations compose, and what is preserved, changed, or lost?
- Are there different views of the same underlying thing?
- Can a local result move into a larger context without changing meaning?
- Where does translation require an explicit witness?
- Which transformations are reversible, lossy, partial, or context-dependent?
- Does a structure factor through a simpler one?
- Are two things equivalent for this consumer without being identical?
- Does combining views expose a distinction neither exposes alone?
- What boundaries prevent one relation type from being treated as another?

Use distinctions only when they clarify the task:

- object versus relation;
- relation versus transformation;
- composition versus aggregation;
- local versus global structure;
- identity versus equivalence;
- preservation versus loss;
- observation versus operational transport;
- representation versus the represented thing;
- schema-level structure versus instance-level state.

## Forbidden jumps

Do not infer:

- composability because operations occur consecutively;
- equivalence from superficial similarity;
- reversibility from successful forward translation;
- operational authority from observational refinement;
- preservation from translation alone;
- a universal abstraction from one local witness;
- categorical structure merely because a diagram can be drawn.

## Return

Return a few load-bearing findings covering:

- relevant entities or kinds of thing;
- load-bearing relations and transformations;
- composition or transport questions;
- what appears preserved or lost;
- structural boundaries;
- hypotheses or open questions requiring witnesses.

Use ordinary domain language unless formal vocabulary increases precision.
Write concise notes with the evidence basis and uncertainty; compactness must
not remove material needed downstream.
