Inventory, but teachable

Stop rereading the whole repo.

Inventory turns useful source-backed knowledge into tiny reusable cards an agent can query fast, validate, and hand off without pretending the card is the final truth.

The promise: less rediscovery, more traceable reuse.

The problem

Raw search is a memory treadmill.

Every agent run can find files, but it often has to reconstruct the same concepts, decisions, and caveats again.

Too broad

A grep result gives matching text, not a reusable explanation for the current task.

Too forgetful

If the agent learned why a detail matters yesterday, that learning may not compound today.

Too risky

Summaries drift when they lose source refs, authority level, and open questions.

The core idea

Inventory is a source-backed knowledge layer.

It sits between raw repository files and higher-level sigils like Context Builder, Invoke, Ontology Vault, Definitions Governance, and Repository Harness.

Raw source

Files, logs, specs, decisions.

Evidence cards

Atomic, reviewable captured knowledge.

Index

Fast lookup by type, tag, source, authority, owner.

Retrieval

Task-shaped selection plus exclusions.

Handoff

Candidate evidence for downstream owners.

The object

An evidence-card is small on purpose.

It is not a wiki page. It is a compact, source-backed unit with enough metadata to be trusted, queried, and challenged.

Atomic

One card should answer one bounded evidence need.

Traceable

Material claims point back to selectors, usually line spans.

Governed

Promotion owner and authority level stop accidental canonization.

A good card feels like a labeled specimen: small enough to inspect, complete enough to reuse.
How querying works

The agent asks for a task, not a dump.

Retrieval should return a compact working set: selected cards, excluded matches, trace notes, open questions, and handoff candidates.

Query

What do I need to decide or build?

Filter

Type, tags, source, authority, owner.

Select

Cards that fit the task.

Explain

Why included, why excluded, what remains open.

This is why shell plus jq is the first surface: fast, local, scriptable, and agent-friendly.
The boundary

Inventory captures evidence. It does not crown reality.

This is the important teaching point: Inventory can prepare evidence for governance, but downstream systems own promotion.

Inventory

Captures source-backed evidence, validates card shape, builds lookup results.

Ontology Vault

May accept or reject candidate relations.

Definitions

May turn term evidence into canonical language.

Context Builder

May compose cards into a task-ready context pack.

The spicy open question

Do we need EvidenceSet?

An EvidenceSet is a candidate grouped read model: not a replacement for cards, and not canonical yet.

First candidate

evidence-set.evidenceset-need

  • Useful for explaining why grouped evidence may matter.
  • Light signal: it proved the shape, not the commitment.
  • Kept candidate-only.
Craft stressor

evidence-set.craft-recursive-ledger

  • Stronger signal: nested contexts, artifacts, gates, blockers, enablers.
  • Shows groups can preserve relationships that independent cards lose.
  • Still needs a minimal schema before promotion.
What to tell your friend

The mental model is a library desk.

Inventory is not the author of every book. It is the desk that labels useful excerpts, keeps their shelf locations, and hands the right bundle to the right specialist.

Card

A labeled excerpt with provenance and authority state.

Index

The catalog that lets an agent find the right excerpt quickly.

Handoff

The note to a specialist saying: here is evidence, please govern it.

Current verdict

The idea makes sense. Refine one part next.

Evidence-cards are ready as the core Inventory shape. EvidenceSet is promising, but should stay candidate-only until its minimal schema is designed and tested.
